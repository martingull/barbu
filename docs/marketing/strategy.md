# Barbu Market Entry and Distribution Strategy

Date: November 4, 2026. Status: working plan, not a launched campaign.
Product owner and proposed campaign lead: Martin. Outreach, spending and public
posts require his approval; this document does not send messages or book ads.

## Decisions and Positioning

**Learn here. Play together.**

Barbu helps people learn a new card game before joining a real table. Short
interactive lessons, feedback and guided hands build confidence; single-player
Play provides practice before games with friends, family or a local card club.
Club membership is not required. The King of Cards is the teacher and brand
identity, not a separate influencer project.

Decisions agreed with Martin:

- Keep the current experience free and without ads while building an audience.
- Prioritise useful learning and repeat use over immediate monetisation.
- Keep the distinction between computer opponents and in-person social play
  explicit. Do not imply online multiplayer.
- Describe Get out and play as an external Google Maps search, not a verified
  club directory, membership service or booking platform.
- Do not promise that all future content will be free forever.

The following audiences, channels and targets are proposed experiments, not
established demand, agreed expenditures or revenue forecasts.

## Initial Audience and Markets

| Priority | Audience | Need | Initial offer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Beginners invited to a family, friend or community card table | Understand the game without feeling put on the spot | Learn Hearts or Gin Rummy, then practise a full hand |
| Secondary | Returning players and people who know one game | Refresh skills or discover another game | A short learning path with explanations and repeatable practice |
| Distribution partners | Club organisers, teachers, libraries and community activity hosts | Help newcomers prepare between meetings | A relevant learning path to assess and optionally recommend |

Begin with English-language materials and reachable contacts in Norway and the
Netherlands, plus a small English-speaking online audience. This is an outreach
focus based on access, not evidence that these countries have the highest demand.
Do not claim Norwegian or Dutch localisation. Test wider English-language reach
before investing in translation or market-specific advertising.

Lead initial content with Hearts and Gin Rummy. Present the wider catalog as a
reason to explore, rather than asking a beginner to evaluate every game at once.
Bridge remains available but should not be advertised as a substitute for a
teacher or as equivalent to established specialist training tools. Ask club
organisers to assess compatibility with their own rules and conventions.

## Competition and Evidence

This is a November 4 research snapshot of seven relevant products/product
families, not a census or an estimate of the total number of competitors. It
includes learning-focused products and adjacent play-first alternatives. Web
rules, videos, books and in-person teaching are additional substitutes.

| Product | Relevant competition | Publicly visible business model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tricky Bridge | Structured beginner lessons, characters and robot practice | Free lessons/practice; paid tournament deals and subscriptions ([site](https://www.trickybridge.com/), [store purchases](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/tricky-bridge-learn-play/id1437652160)) |
| Funbridge | Bridge play, learning and improvement tools | Premium subscriptions, with analysis and commented deals in higher tiers ([official explanation](https://funbridge.com/blog/en/premium-plus/)) |
| NeuralPlay | Separate games with hints, review and adjustable opponents | Advertising and paid ad removal ([Hearts listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.neuralplay.android.hearts)) |
| CardGames.io | Broad classic-game collection and straightforward play | Advertising and an ad-free mobile subscription ([subscription terms](https://cardgames.io/app-premium-eula/)) |
| Trickster Cards | Multi-game practice, multiplayer and clubs | Ads, competition chips and non-renewing prepaid VIP passes ([pricing](https://www.trickstercards.com/features/free-vs-vip/), [VIP terms](https://www.trickstercards.com/help/about-vip-pass/)) |
| CardzMania | Broad multiplayer and computer-opponent catalog | Advertises no ads or subscriptions; funding not established in this research ([site](https://www.cardzmania.com/)) |
| Card Games Academy | Multi-game explanations and guided learning activities | Paid offering not established from reviewed pages ([learning approach](https://cardgamesacademy.com/how-to-play/)) |

Public Google Play listings reviewed showed 100K+ downloads for
[Tricky Bridge](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.Forklift.TrickyBridge),
500K+ for NeuralPlay Hearts, and 1M+ for
[NeuralPlay Bridge](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.neuralplay.android.bridge).
These are cumulative Android download bands, not active users, paying users or
evidence of profitability. Reliable product-level revenue and profit figures
were not established. Recheck pricing and offers before using comparisons publicly.

Our hypothesis is that a consistent beginner journey across several games,
with explanations and preparation for in-person play, can attract a useful niche.
We have not established market size, market share or an uncontested category.
Being free is an acquisition aid, not a durable differentiator by itself.

## Acquisition Channels

### 1. Organisers and Teachers

Proposed lead: Martin. Build a private list of 20 relevant organisers using
public organisation contact channels or introductions. Start with people able
to assess Hearts or Gin Rummy; do not send an indiscriminate bridge-club mailing.
Ask for three pilot groups, not an endorsement before they have tried Barbu.

Offer a short demonstration and one specific learning path to try before their
next meeting. Ask where the rules differ and where beginners get stuck. Provide
a listing link or QR code only after public availability is verified. Request
permission before using a club's name, logo, feedback or testimonial publicly.

Suggested message, to customise rather than mass-send:

> I am building Barbu to help beginners learn card games before joining a real
> table. Would you be willing to try the [game] learning path and tell me whether
> it would help your newcomers? It currently has no ads or charges. I would be
> happy to give a short demonstration; there is no obligation to recommend it.

Keep personal contact details and interview notes out of this public repository.
Respect a refusal; do not automate unsolicited bulk messages.

### 2. Useful Short Videos

Proposed lead: Martin, with tooling assistance for drafts. Produce six distinct
shorts over the experiment, beginning with the two existing
[pilot scripts](shorts.json). A cross-post is a second placement, not new creative.
Use one real decision, a pause, a concise explanation and a truthful call to action.
The [shorts pilot](README.md) owns capture, preview and publication checks.

Suggested subjects: Hearts follow-suit, avoiding a penalty, passing three; Gin
melds, deadwood and knocking. Check each against the published build. Test
TikTok and YouTube Shorts without paid boosting. No separate character account
or cinematic campaign is needed to test whether teaching content attracts users.

### 3. Relevant Communities

Proposed lead: Martin. Select three communities with an appropriate audience
and promotion rules. Ask moderators where necessary, disclose authorship and
offer a useful demonstration or answer. Ask a specific learning question rather
than repeatedly posting a download link. No bought or incentivised reviews.

### 4. Store Discovery and Word of Mouth

Keep store copy and screenshots aligned with learning before in-person play.
Use the existing Share Barbu action; ask pilot users whether they actually
recommended it, rather than assuming a share action creates an install.
Game-specific search pages and custom store listings are possible later tests,
not existing capabilities or prerequisites for the first campaign.

## Four-Week Experiment

Start week one after public Android availability is verified. Martin owns the
proposed schedule; no external activity has been scheduled by this plan.

| Week | Proposed work | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Verify listing/install, prepare outreach list, contact first five organisers, observe first three beginners, publish first two shorts | Installation friction, first-lesson confusion, channel baselines |
| 2 | Contact ten more organisers, run pilot demos where accepted, publish two more shorts, make approved community posts | Replies, accepted pilots, lesson-to-hand transitions, follow-up feedback |
| 3 | Contact remaining five organisers, publish final two shorts, reach ten observed beginners | Repeat use in eligible early cohorts, learning problems, actual in-person use |
| 4 | Complete seven-day follow-ups, compare channels and decide next experiment | Returning users, organiser recommendations, acquisition evidence and time spent |

Proposed cash acquisition budget: **EUR 0** for this experiment. No paid reach,
testing-provider recruitment, influencer fees or sponsorship purchases. Any
optional expense needs a separate budget decision. Allow roughly 4-6 hours a
week of Martin's time; this excludes development fixes and is a planning allowance.

Working goals, not forecasts or industry benchmarks:

- 100 new genuine users during the experiment, excluding paid testers and our devices.
- 30% of measurable users in cohorts with a complete seven-day observation window
  return at least once during days 1-7. This is seven-day return, not day-7 retention.
- Three organisers willing to recommend Barbu after trying it.
- Ten beginner observations/interviews, with permission and a follow-up invitation.

Do not report "30 returning users out of 100" unless all 100 had a full follow-up
window and we can actually measure their return. An unmeasurable target remains
unmeasured, not zero or a guessed success.

## Measurement Without Inventing Analytics

Gameplay and learning progress are currently local. Central lesson events and
cross-channel attribution are not implemented. Start with the metrics actually
available in Play Console, platform post statistics and voluntary pilot follow-ups.

| Question | Initial measurement | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Are people finding and installing Barbu? | Console store visitors, acquisitions and available source breakdowns | Record exact metric definitions; downloads are not unique active users |
| Which content attracts interest? | Per-post views, watch time and link clicks where available | Views and clicks cannot be converted into assumed installs |
| Do beginners understand the lesson? | Observe ten volunteers completing a lesson and starting a hand | Small, selected sample; report numerator and denominator |
| Do people return? | Available Console retention measures, separately labelled; voluntary seven-day pilot follow-up | Pilot replies are self-selected; nonresponses are unknown |
| Does learning lead to real-world play? | Ask whether they played the game with another person | Club-search clicks do not establish attendance or in-person play |
| Is a channel worth repeating? | Outcomes alongside time spent, channel and dates | Organic outreach costs time even with zero cash spend |

## Decision Rules and Monetisation

At the four-week review, diagnose before expanding:

- Few visits: test audience, outreach and the opening message before adding games.
- Visits without installs: inspect availability, listing clarity and trust signals.
- Installs without learning progress: observe onboarding and fix demonstrated friction.
- Lesson completion without return: ask whether users achieved their one-off goal,
  got stuck or lacked a reason to continue; lower retention alone does not prove failure.
- Organisers recommending it: repeat the successful partnership format before
  considering paid acquisition. Document actual evidence, not just positive replies.

Remain free and ad-free through this initial experiment. Revisit monetisation
once we have repeat-use evidence and understand what users value, not merely when
an install counter reaches a number. Possible later experiments are restrained
ads in ordinary Play with permanent ad removal, substantial paid learning packs,
or subscriptions backed by recurring value. None is approved or implemented here.
Protect lessons and guided explanations; do not create cooldowns or charge for
correcting mistakes. Club licences would require a validated organiser need.

Before spending on acquisition, agree a capped experiment and a measurable
activation outcome. Scale only with evidence about retention and sustainable
economics; competitor download totals cannot supply our conversion or revenue assumptions.
